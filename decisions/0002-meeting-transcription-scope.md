\# 0002 — Is meeting summarisation within Annex III point 4(b)?



\*\*Status:\*\* accepted · \*\*Date:\*\* 2026-08-23



\## Question

The pilot sponsor argues that SYS-004 only produces a record of what was said and

performs no assessment of any employee, so the Art. 6(3) "preparatory task" ground

applies. Managers have, however, started pasting the generated summaries into

performance review notes.



\## Options

1\. Accept the derogation. The system stays outside Chapter III; we document the

&#x20;  Art. 6(3) assessment and register under Art. 6(4). Lightest option, but it rests

&#x20;  on a description of the system that no longer matches how it is used.

2\. Treat as Annex III point 4(b) high-risk. Full deployer obligations under Art. 26

&#x20;  from 2 December 2027, including informing workers and their representatives.



\## Decision

Option 2, on two independent grounds.



First, the summaries attribute contributions to named individuals and characterise

what each person committed to. That is an evaluation of personal aspects relating to

performance at work, and therefore profiling within GDPR Art. 4(4), which the AI Act

adopts. Art. 6(3) makes the derogation unavailable in every case where the system

performs profiling of natural persons, so the sponsor's argument cannot succeed even

if it is otherwise well made.



Second, and independently, intended purpose under Art. 3(12) is the use for which the

system is put into service, not the use described in the vendor's materials. The system

is in fact feeding performance evaluation. Even if the profiling analysis were wrong,

the drift in actual use brings it within point 4(b).



\## Consequence

Deployer obligations under Art. 26 apply: use per the instructions for use, competent

human oversight, input data relevance, log retention, and the Art. 26(7) duty to inform

affected workers and their representatives before putting the system into use. A DPIA is

required under GDPR Art. 35. Because the system generates synthetic content, Art. 50(2)

marking obligations also apply on the transparency timeline. The pilot should not move to

general availability until the Art. 26(7) notification has been made.



\## What would change this

Two changes together, not either alone: a hard control preventing summaries from entering

performance management processes, and removal of per-speaker attribution so output is a

record of decisions rather than of individuals. That would be a materially different

system and would need a new register entry, not an amendment to this one.
